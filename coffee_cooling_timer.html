This timer is powered by Newton's Law of Cooling — a first-order differential equation. Here's how we go from the physics principle all the way to the formula your coffee uses.

1

Start with the base differential equation. The rate of temperature change is proportional to the difference between the object's temperature T and the surrounding room temperature Troom.

dT/dt = k(T − Troom)
2

Separate variables — move all T terms to the left, all t terms to the right:

dT / (T − Troom) = k dt
3

Integrate both sides:

∫ dT/(T − Troom) = ∫ k dt
4

Left side integrates to a natural log; right side to kt + C:

ln|T − Troom| = kt + C
5

Exponentiate both sides to remove the natural log:

T − Troom = ekt+C = C1ekt
6

Apply the initial condition: at t = 0, T = T0, so C1 = T0 − Troom. This gives the standard formula:

T(t) = Troom + (T0 − Troom) · e−kt
7

To find k from your calibration reading TM at time tM, rearrange and apply the natural logarithm:

k = −ln[(TM − Troom) / (T0 − Troom)] / tM
